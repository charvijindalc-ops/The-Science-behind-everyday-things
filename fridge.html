What is a Refrigerator?



A refrigerator is an appliance that keeps food and drinks at a low temperature.

But a refrigerator does not simply "produce cold."

Instead, it uses a refrigeration system to remove heat from inside the refrigerator and release that heat into the surroundings.

This process continuously keeps the inside of the refrigerator cool.


🧊 The Science Behind Refrigeration

A common refrigerator uses a vapor-compression refrigeration cycle.

A special fluid called a refrigerant moves through the system. It changes between liquid and gas as it circulates through different components.

The four important parts are:

1. Compressor
2. Condenser
3. Expansion Device
4. Evaporator

Diagram of a basic refrigerator refrigeration cycle.


⚙️ Four Main Parts

Component What Does It Do?
Compressor The compressor compresses the refrigerant gas and increases its pressure and temperature.
Condenser The refrigerant releases heat to the surroundings and changes from a gas into a liquid.
Expansion Device The refrigerant experiences a drop in pressure and becomes much colder.
Evaporator The cold refrigerant absorbs heat from inside the refrigerator and evaporates into a gas.

🔥 Why Is the Back of a Fridge Warm?

Have you ever noticed that the back or sides of a refrigerator can feel warm?

This happens because the refrigerator must release the heat that it removed from inside.

The condenser transfers this heat to the surrounding air.

So, a warm back or side is actually part of the normal refrigeration process.


🔬 The Complete Process

Step What Happens?
1 The compressor compresses the refrigerant gas.
2 The refrigerant releases heat through the condenser.
3 The refrigerant passes through an expansion device and its pressure drops.
4 The cold refrigerant absorbs heat inside the refrigerator.
5 The refrigerant returns to the compressor and the cycle starts again.
